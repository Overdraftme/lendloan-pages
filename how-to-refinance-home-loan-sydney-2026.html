Refinancing your home loan is one of the highest-return financial moves an Australian homeowner can make. In 2026, there's a very good chance your current lender is no longer giving you their best deal. This guide covers everything you need to know about refinancing your Sydney home loan.

What Is Refinancing and Why Does It Matter?

Refinancing means replacing your existing home loan with a new one — either with your existing lender (internal refinance) or a new lender (external refinance). The goal is usually a lower rate, better features, or access to equity.

On a $700,000 Sydney loan, reducing your rate by just 0.5% saves approximately $3,500 per year — $17,500 over five years.

Signs It's Time to Refinance

  • You haven't reviewed your rate in the past 12–18 months
  • You're on your lender's standard variable rate — not a discounted rate
  • You've built up more than 20% equity in your property
  • Your fixed rate period is about to expire
  • You want to access equity for renovations, investment, or other purposes
  • Your financial situation has improved significantly since you took out the loan

What Does Refinancing Cost?

  • Discharge fee from your existing lender: typically $150–$400
  • Government registration fees: typically $150–$300
  • Application fee with new lender: $0–$600 (many waive this)
  • Valuation fee: $0–$300 (many lenders cover this)
  • Break costs if on a fixed rate — can be significant

Cashback Deals — Are They Worth It?

Many lenders offer cashback deals for refinancers — typically $2,000–$4,000. These can be genuine value, but are often paired with slightly higher rates that erode the benefit over time.

We always calculate the total cost of ownership over 3–5 years — not just the upfront cashback — to determine the true best value.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often can I refinance?

There's no legal limit, but most brokers recommend reviewing your loan every 2–3 years at minimum. Refinancing too frequently can trigger multiple credit enquiries and repeated costs.

Will refinancing hurt my credit score?

Every loan application triggers a credit enquiry which can temporarily reduce your score slightly. Refinancing once every 2–3 years has minimal long-term credit impact.

Can I refinance if my property value has dropped?

It depends on how much it's dropped and your current loan balance. If your LVR has risen above 80%, you may need to pay LMI to refinance. We'll always explore every option available.
