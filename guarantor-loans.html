What Is a Guarantor Home Loan?

In a guarantor loan, a family member — most commonly parents — puts up equity in their existing property as additional security for your loan. This means you can borrow up to 105% of the purchase price without a 20% deposit and without paying LMI. The guarantee is typically limited to the shortfall amount and can be released once you've built sufficient equity — usually when your LVR reaches 80%.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the risks for the guarantor?

The guarantor's property is at risk if the borrower defaults. We always recommend both parties seek independent legal advice and structure the guarantee as limited (not unlimited) and to the minimum amount required.

Can parents in another state be a guarantor?

Yes — the guarantor's property needs to be in Australia. Interstate guarantors are common and most lenders accommodate them with some additional legal steps.

When can the guarantee be released?

Usually when your LVR reaches 80% — through property price growth, loan repayments, or both. We monitor this and alert you when eligible.
