Buying your first home in NSW is a massive milestone — and the government wants to help you get there. The NSW First Home Owner Grant is one of several schemes that can put thousands of dollars back in your pocket. But the rules can be confusing and the eligibility criteria specific. This guide covers everything you need to know about the FHOG in 2026.

What Is the NSW First Home Owner Grant?

The NSW First Home Owner Grant is a one-time payment from the NSW government for eligible first home buyers purchasing or building a new residential property. As of 2026, the grant amount is $10,000 for new homes across NSW.

Note: The FHOG applies to NEW homes only. If you're buying an established property, you won't receive the grant — but you may be eligible for stamp duty concessions.

Who Is Eligible for the NSW FHOG in 2026?

To qualify for the $10,000 FHOG in NSW, you must meet all of the following:

  • Australian citizen or permanent resident aged 18 or over
  • You or your partner have never previously owned residential property in Australia
  • Purchasing or building a NEW home (not an established property)
  • Total property value must not exceed $600,000 for purchase, or $750,000 for a contract to build
  • You must intend to live in the property for at least 12 months commencing within 12 months of settlement

Stamp Duty Concessions — A Separate Benefit

Separate to the FHOG, NSW first home buyers may qualify for stamp duty concessions:

  • Full stamp duty exemption on properties valued up to $800,000
  • Partial stamp duty concession on properties valued between $800,000 and $1,000,000
  • This applies to BOTH new and established properties — unlike the FHOG

💡 First home buyers can potentially save $40,000–$76,000 combining the $10,000 FHOG, stamp duty exemption, and 5% Deposit Scheme (no LMI) on eligible purchases.

The Federal 5% Deposit Scheme — Stack It With the FHOG

The First Home Guarantee allows eligible first home buyers to purchase with as little as a 5% deposit, with the Federal Government guaranteeing the remaining 15% — meaning you avoid LMI entirely.

You can combine the First Home Guarantee with the NSW FHOG if buying a new home — giving you both a $10,000 grant AND no LMI.

How to Claim the NSW First Home Owner Grant

You can claim the FHOG in two ways:

  • Through an approved lender — the grant is included in your loan settlement, so you don't need to find the funds separately
  • Directly through Revenue NSW — submit your application within 12 months of settlement or construction completion

Common Reasons FHOG Claims Are Rejected

  • Buying an established property assuming the FHOG applies — it doesn't
  • One applicant previously owned property in their own name
  • Not moving into the property within 12 months of settlement
  • Property value exceeding the threshold
  • Purchasing in a trust or company name

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I get the FHOG on a house and land package?

Yes — house and land packages typically qualify because you're building a new home. The total value of land plus construction contract must be under $750,000.

Does my partner's history affect my eligibility?

Yes. If your partner has previously owned residential property in Australia, neither of you will be eligible for the FHOG as a couple.

How long does it take to receive the grant?

When claimed through your lender, the grant is typically applied at settlement. When claimed directly through Revenue NSW, it can take 5–14 business days after assessment.
