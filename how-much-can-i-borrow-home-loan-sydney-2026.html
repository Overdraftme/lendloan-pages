What Determines Your Borrowing Capacity?

  • Your gross income — salary, wages, bonuses, overtime, rental income, dividends
  • Your existing debts — car loans, personal loans, credit card limits, HECS/HELP debt
  • Your living expenses — assessed against the Household Expenditure Measure (HEM)
  • The loan's interest rate — lenders add a mandatory 3% buffer on top of the actual rate
  • The loan term — standard 30-year terms maximise borrowing capacity
  • The number of dependants — each child reduces assessed borrowing capacity

The 3% Buffer — Why You Can't Borrow as Much as the Rate Suggests

APRA requires all lenders to assess your repayment ability at your loan's actual rate PLUS a 3% buffer. If your loan rate is 6.2%, lenders assess whether you could make repayments at 9.2% — significantly reducing the amount you can borrow.

How to Maximise Your Borrowing Capacity

  • Pay down or close credit cards — even unused credit limits reduce your capacity
  • Reduce your credit card limits — lenders assess the limit, not the balance
  • Consolidate existing debts before applying
  • Choose the right lender — borrowing capacity varies by up to 30% between lenders for the same income
  • Ensure all income is accounted for — bonuses, overtime, rental income, allowances

Why Borrowing Capacity Varies by Lender

Two major banks can have dramatically different borrowing capacity assessments for the same borrower — because lenders use different expense benchmarks, treat income differently, and have different policies on credit cards and HECS debt.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does HECS/HELP debt reduce my borrowing capacity?

Yes — lenders include your HECS repayment amount as committed expenditure, which reduces your assessable income. This is especially relevant for nurses, teachers, and medical professionals.

Can I borrow more by going with a non-bank lender?

In some cases, yes. Non-bank and specialist lenders can sometimes offer higher borrowing capacity — particularly for self-employed borrowers or those with complex income structures.

How accurate are online borrowing calculators?

Online calculators give a rough guide but can be significantly wrong as they can't account for your specific income structure or the lender's specific policies. A free broker assessment is far more accurate.
